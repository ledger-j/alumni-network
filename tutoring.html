Student tutoring

Stuck on a course? Ask someone who took it before.

Tutoring on UniCircle is peer help with a course. Advanced students and alumni offer to explain what they know; students in their tutorial groups ask for exactly that. Small, specific, and free for students.

Mentoring or tutoring?

Two kinds of help, two different questions.

Mentoring

An alumnus guides a career.

About choices after the degree: a first job, a sector, a city to move to. One short call, a CV read or a coffee. How mentoring works.

Tutoring

A peer helps with a course.

About the course you are taking right now: a concept that will not click, how to approach a tutorial assignment, what an exam asks for. The helper is an advanced student or an alumnus who took the course before.

How it works

Three steps, inside your circle.

Tutoring follows the same small-ask pattern as mentoring, built around tutorial groups. It is opt-in on both sides.

  1. Offer or requestAn advanced student or alumnus says which course they can help with. A student says which course they need help with, and what exactly.
  2. Get matched in the circleOffers and requests meet around the same course and programme, so people from your own tutorial groups come first.
  3. Chat and meetTalk it through in UniCircle messages, then agree how to meet: online, or in person if you are in the same city.

Prototype: this page describes what we are building and testing in waves. Details may change as real students and alumni try it.

Ground rules

Help that stays small and clear.

  • Opt-in on both sides. Nobody is listed as a tutor unless they switch it on.
  • Specific asks. A course and a question beat "can you help me study".
  • Messages stay in UniCircle. Contact details are not shared unless both people choose to.
  • Raise a concern. If anything feels wrong, write to hello@unicircle.eu.

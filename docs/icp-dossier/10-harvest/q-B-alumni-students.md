You are a researcher on alumni engagement and student behaviour. I need in-depth, evidence-based profiles of the people who would use an alumni network, so a product team can design for them. Use Deep Research. Context: Netherlands first (Maastricht business-school setting), international alumni.

## Product (context only)
UniCircle: a lifelong alumni network: member directory, a city-level map, mentoring and student tutoring, live chat, and **status points**: students and alumni earn points by tutoring, mentoring and giving feedback; points unlock the right to approach others; high-value alumni set rules for who may contact them (so they are not spammed as on LinkedIn).

## Part 1: four alumni types
B1 finance (banking, asset management, audit, fintech) · B2 management (general management, operations, entrepreneurs, corporate leadership) · B3 law (corporate and tax lawyers, in-house counsel, legal advisers) · B4 consultancy (strategy and management consulting, Big Four advisory).
For EACH type, return:
1. Who they are 5, 10 and 20 years after graduation: typical roles, seniority, employers, cities (Dutch and international hubs)
2. What they want from an alumni network (ranked) and what they refuse
3. Inbox protection: how they behave on LinkedIn and alumni platforms, response patterns to cold requests, what makes them say yes to mentoring or a student call (time boxes, format, proof of effort)
4. Their incentives to give (reputation, hiring pipeline, giving back, CPD hours, firm CSR hours) with evidence
5. Objections and risks (time, confidentiality, compliance rules for finance and law, conflict of interest)
6. Which UniCircle mechanics fit: status points, contact rules, map, tutoring/mentoring, group chat, events per day
7. Evidence quality per claim: study, survey, benchmark or practitioner opinion, with year

## Part 2: three student types
Working model: **red** = action-oriented (decides fast, wants results, competitive), **blue** = thinker (analytical, wants depth and accuracy, risk-averse), **social** = connector (relationship-driven, group-oriented, values belonging). 
1. State clearly what the scientific standing of colour/DISC-style typologies is (validity, reliability, criticism) and what a product team can still legitimately use them for. Do NOT present them as fact about individuals.
2. For each type, describe observable behaviour in an academic and career setting that a platform can detect (what they click first, what they ask for, how they study, how they ask for help), not personality claims.
3. What each type needs from tutoring, mentoring, peer groups, internships and events; how each reacts to points, rankings and gamification (motivation, risk of harm)
4. How a platform should adapt (onboarding questions, tone, features per type) with examples from learning-science or student-engagement research
5. Dutch and Maastricht context: problem-based learning, tutorial groups, 4,200-student faculty size, international student share

## Rules
- Real sources only; a source URL or full citation for EVERY claim. No invented studies, statistics, authors or URLs. If unsure write UNVERIFIED and why.
- Separate: (a) evidence, (b) inference, (c) opinion. Label each.
- No marketing adjectives, no generic trend paragraphs.
- No named private individuals. Segment-level only.
- Output: one block per type, then a comparison table (type | wants | refuses | best mechanic | evidence strength). End with "Gaps".

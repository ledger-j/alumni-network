# UniCircle: Target Group Briefing, Segment C
## Persona: Students who seek tutoring, internships and peer groups, shown as three behaviour modes (red, blue, social)
Segment state: demand side of mentoring and tutoring; strong group identity in problem-based settings.
Proof basis: P2 (tutoring requests, group channels), P1 (tutorial-group spillover) · Confidence: low-med · Built: 2026-11-24 · Rounds: Claude web r1; Gemini cross-examination pending

Source files: `10-harvest/claude-r1-B-alumni-students.md`, `20-distilled/fact-cards-B.md`, `data.json`. Labels: E evidence, I inference, O opinion. Everything about colour types is a working heuristic.

---
## Purpose
Describe three modes as hypotheses to test with behaviour data, never as labels on people.

> **Caveat (read first).** Colour/DISC typologies have weak scientific standing. Independent evidence for predictive validity is weak and reliability reports come mainly from vendors (E, low-med, Wikipedia summary of critics). The similar MBTI was judged by a 1993 review to fall short of test standards (E, med). The closest education analogue, learning-style matching, has no adequate evidence base (E, high, Pashler et al. 2008). No source links a DISC type to platform behaviour (UNVERIFIED). A colour label must not be treated as a fact about a person, gate access, be shown to others, or be used for selection or grading. Legitimate uses: design personas as hypotheses; segment by observable behaviour and call it a "mode"; let users self-select a preference they can change; offer all options to all users. Whether inferred personality data raises GDPR issues was not researched (gap G-B6).

## 1. Who They Are (Firmographic Context & DMU Baseline)
- E: More than 50% of UM students come from abroad. SBE tutorial groups hold 8 to 15 students plus a tutor. SBE size is conflicting (6,500+ on the SBE page versus 4,200+ in Times Higher Education): UNVERIFIED, do not size from it.
- I: Group identity is strong: "who was in your tutorial group" is a built-in hook; mixed-nationality groups can lower fit for some students (abstract only).
- E: Avoidant and executive (get-the-answer) help-seeking relate negatively to achievement, instrumental help-seeking positively (small effects); students prefer informal friends over formal tutors (meta-analysis, 108 studies, 2023).
- DMU: not applicable. The university alumni office is the buyer (Segment A).

## 2. Psychology and Emotional Architecture
Self-determination theory (E, high): competence, autonomy and relatedness are basic needs; thwarting them lowers motivation.

| Mode | Observable behaviour (I, hypotheses) | Wants | Refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red (action-oriented) | First click on "find a mentor / internship / who works at X" or a message button; outcome-first asks; skips profile; short sessions; repeats if a result arrives within a day | Fast results, outcome intros, one concrete ask answered within 48h, project groups with a deadline, pitch nights | Long forms, slow replies |
| Blue (thinker) | Reads how points and contact rules work; checks mentor profile depth; drafts and abandons; reads the FAQ first; long sessions, few messages | Depth, accuracy, clear rules, explanations with sources, a written agenda, seated events with Q&A | Public ranking, vague asks |
| Social (connector) | First click on tutorial-group or city group, people tab, chat; asks who else is going; high message volume; low formal-request conversion unless introduced | Belonging, group tutoring and peer-buddy, group mentoring and alumni "office hours" with 5-8 students, tutorial-group channels | Solitary flows, public rank |

### Points and rankings
- E: Gamification effects on learning are small; stable for cognitive outcomes, less stable for motivational and behavioural; competition combined with collaboration worked best for behaviour (meta-analysis, abstract via summary).
- E: In one 16-week course (N about 71), leaderboard plus badges lowered intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and exam scores.
- I per mode: red may respond to visible rank but ranking can crowd out autonomy and invite gaming (quantity over quality of tutoring); blue is most likely to feel anxiety from public rank, so show private progress; social responds to team or group points and thank-you signals, but public ranking can exclude low-ranked members and promote cliques.

## 3. Value Proposition Mapping (proof-anchored)
| UniCircle capability | Proof | Mode | Adaptation (I) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mentoring request with one concrete ask | P2 | Red | Onboarding "What do you want to do in the next 4 weeks?" with 3 outcome buttons; task-style progress; team leaderboards, not individual |
| Message templates, "preview what the mentor sees", contact-rule transparency | P2 | Blue | Precise tone, reason for each field, private progress, no countdown timers |
| Tutorial-group and city channels, group mentoring | P1, P2 | Social | Onboarding "Which tutorial group / city / society are you in?"; invite-a-friend; group streaks |
### Positioning rules
- Never say "we know your type": say "pick how you want to start".
- Tutoring for red: design the tutor to ask one reversible question so quick answers do not become executive help-seeking.

## 8. Trust Vulnerability
- Colour labels shown to others or stored as a person attribute would undercut trust and may raise data-protection questions (UNVERIFIED legal view). Keep any mode a self-selected, changeable preference.
- Gaming risk: points for tutoring can be farmed with low-quality sessions (I).

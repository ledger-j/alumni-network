For alumni

Somebody once answered your questions. Mentoring on UniCircle lets you do the same without signing up for a programme or a monthly commitment. Flip one switch, write a single line about what you can offer, and decide each request on its own merits.

  • You set the scope. Your one-line offer tells students exactly what to ask for.
  • Requests, not cold pitches. Each one arrives as a message with a clear goal.
  • Pause any time. Busy quarter? Switch it off; switch it back on later.
How it works

Four steps, no programme to enrol in.

Mentoring lives inside your UniCircle profile. It's off until you turn it on — on both sides.

  1. An alumnus opts inThey flip "open to mentor" and write a one-line offer — for example, "Happy to do a 20-minute call on moving into consulting."
  2. A student browsesMentors can be filtered by programme, sector and city. Open mentors also show up on the map, by city and neighbourhood only.
  3. The student sends a requestThey choose a goal and a format — intro call, CV review, coffee chat or message — and add a short note. It arrives as a message.
  4. The alumnus decidesThey reply when it suits them — yes, a different format, or a polite no. The conversation stays in UniCircle messages unless both choose otherwise.
The "small asks" principle

A scoped offer is easier to say yes to.

"Will you be my mentor?" is a big question. It implies months of commitment, and many busy people say no — or say nothing. "Could you look at my CV for the consulting application?" has an obvious shape and an obvious end.

So mentoring on UniCircle is built around small, specific asks. Alumni say what they can give; students ask for that. Longer relationships can still grow from there — they just don't have to be promised on day one.

Ground rules

Generous by choice, safe by design.

  • Opt-in on both sides. Alumni choose to be listed; students choose to reach out.
  • No cold messages. Students can only contact mentors who are open, through a structured request.
  • Easy to pause. The same switch that put you on the list takes you off it.
  • Free for students. No paid tiers stand between a student and a mentor.
  • Raise a concern. If anything feels wrong, write to hello@unicircle.eu and we act on it. One-tap reporting and blocking are being built before launch.

Questions

Mentoring, answered.

How much time does mentoring take?

As much as you offer. Most offers are a single call, one CV read or one coffee. You decide on each request, there's no minimum, and you can pause mentoring whenever you like.

Who can be a mentor?

Any alumnus with a UniCircle profile can switch mentoring on. You don't need a senior title — what students value most is someone who did the same programme or works in the field they're considering.

I graduated recently. Do I have anything to offer?

Yes. Recent graduates are often the most useful person to ask about applications, first jobs, internships and moving to a new city — because they did it a year or two ago. Keep your offer small and specific.

Does it cost anything?

Mentoring is free for students, always. Students never pay to reach a mentor, and alumni are never paid to mentor — it's people giving back to their own community.

What can other people see about me?

Your profile shows what you choose to share. On the map you appear by city and neighbourhood only — never a street address or GPS location. Contact details are not shared; requests and replies happen in UniCircle messages.

What if a request isn't a good fit?

Say so, kindly — or suggest a format that works better for you. A no is a normal part of mentoring, and students can always ask someone else.

Can our university run its own mentoring programme on UniCircle?

That's part of the plan: universities own their alumni network on UniCircle and can encourage mentoring within it — for a faculty, a programme or a city chapter. If you'd like to talk about it, see For universities or write to hello@unicircle.eu.

The problem

You land in a new city knowing nobody — while half your tutorial is a tram ride away.

An exchange semester scatters a cohort across Europe. Someone you sat next to every week might be renting a room three streets from yours, and you would never know.

The group chat doesn't fix it. It splits into a dozen smaller chats, the useful message about a flat or a study room sinks under a hundred others, and nobody can see who is actually where.

UniCircle puts the answer on a map: your course, your tutorial group, your city — by neighbourhood, never by street.

How it works

Four steps from "who else is here?" to a study group.

  1. Add your course and tutorialThe courses you take and the tutorial group you meet in each week. That's what links you to the people you already know.
  2. Choose your cityWhere you're going this term. Add a neighbourhood if you like — it helps people one district over find you. Never a street.
  3. See your groupYour tutorial group appears as blue dots: who is in the same city, who is in the next district, who is arriving later.
  4. Message and planStart a study session, share a flat tip, find a travel buddy for the weekend. Red dots show alumni nearby you can ask for local advice.

Privacy for students

Visible to your group. Invisible to everyone else.

Being findable abroad should feel safe. The blue layer is built on the same rules as the rest of the map — and a few stricter ones.

Read the full privacy model of the map.

  • City and neighbourhood only. Your dot is the city you chose, or a neighbourhood if you add one. Never a street, never GPS.
  • Merged with others. People close together share one dot with a count, and the map can't zoom past district level.
  • Tutorial groups stay private. Only members of a tutorial group can see who in that group is where.
  • No live location. The dot moves only when you change it. Nothing tracks you in the background.
  • Leave any time. Remove your city and your dot is gone, for a day or for good.
Questions

Student exchange, answered.

Is it only for students from my university?

Your courses and tutorial groups are your own university's, so the blue dots you see first are the people you study with. Each university runs its own circle on UniCircle; anything shared beyond it is the university's choice, and we'll say clearly what is switched on when yours opens.

What counts as a tutorial group?

The small group you meet with each week for a course — some programmes call it a seminar, workgroup or section. On UniCircle it is the course code plus the group, so classmates find each other without sharing anything else.

Who can see my dot?

Members of your tutorial groups see you under that group, at city or neighbourhood level. Nobody sees a street or a live position, and you can remove your dot whenever you want.

What does it cost?

Nothing. UniCircle is free for students — including the map, your tutorial groups and reaching alumni for advice.

Can I ask alumni in my exchange city for help?

Yes. Red dots are alumni; those who opted in to mentoring show it. Small, specific asks work best: a neighbourhood to live in, a coffee, a view on the local job market. See how mentoring works.

My exchange city isn't on the map yet. What now?

We open city by city. Join the waitlist with the city you're heading to — that tells us where to open next, and you'll be on the map as soon as it does. Without a neighbourhood, you simply appear at city level.

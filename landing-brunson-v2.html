02 · Mentoring

Good advice usually starts with a small, easy yes.

"Will you be my mentor?" is a big question. It implies months of commitment, and many busy people say no — or say nothing. "Could you look at my CV for the consulting application?" has an obvious shape and an obvious end.

So alumni offer one specific thing — a short call, a CV read, a coffee in their city — and students ask for exactly that. No cold messages, and students never pay.

→

For students

Find people who sat in the same lecture halls and now work where you want to go. Filter by the programme you're in, the sector you're curious about, or the city you're moving to — then ask for one concrete thing.

  • Mentors who said yes in advance. Everyone you see has switched mentoring on.
  • A request that's easy to write. Pick a goal and a format; we shape the message.
  • Free, always. Students never pay to reach a mentor.
←

For alumni

Somebody once answered your questions. Mentoring lets you do the same without signing up for a programme or a monthly commitment. Flip one switch, write a single line about what you can offer, and decide each request on its own merits.

  • You set the scope. Your one-line offer tells students exactly what to ask for.
  • Requests, not cold pitches. Each one arrives as a message with a clear goal.
  • Pause any time. Busy quarter? Switch it off; switch it back on later.
The "small asks" principle

A scoped offer is easier to say yes to.

Alumni say what they can give; students ask for that. Longer relationships can still grow from there — they just don't have to be promised on day one.

03 · Why we built this

The best network you'll ever have shouldn't start with a cold message.

We're students and alumni who wanted this for ourselves. The most valuable network you'll ever have is the people who studied where you study, and today reaching them means cold LinkedIn messages that nobody answers.

UniCircle began as a simple idea inside a European business school's alumni office: more value for alumni, through a platform built for them rather than rented from a feed. It soon outgrew a single faculty: an independent platform that connects universities across Europe and strengthens the network in two directions, between alumni, and between alumni and the students coming up behind them.

So we built the warm way in: alumni who choose to help, small, easy asks, and a network opened in small waves, so every conversation is a real one.

The name says the same thing: University and United, drawn into one Circle. A circle has no end, and neither does your relationship with the people you studied with.

06 · Private by design

Privacy isn't a setting here. It's how it's built.

UniCircle only works if people trust it. So every choice is yours, and every default is the private one. Read our privacy policy.

  • City or neighbourhood, never a street. You type a city and, if you like, a neighbourhood. No street, no postcode, no coordinates, no GPS, no live location or movement history — ever.
  • Your dot sits on the area, not on you. It's placed on the neighbourhood (or city) centre, and everyone in the same area merges into one dot with a count. The more people, the less any single person stands out.
  • Zoom stops at district. The map can't zoom to street level. There's nothing finer to see, because nothing finer is stored.
  • Opt-in everything. Mentoring, the map and messages are things you switch on. You appear on the map only once you add a city; leave it empty and you can still use mentoring and everything else.
  • Leave any time. Remove your city and your dot disappears. You can ask us to export or delete your data at any time.
  • Members only. Names behind a dot are visible to signed-in members of your circle — never to the public or search engines. Tutorial groups stay private: only members of a group can see who in it is where.
  • Only what's needed. We ask only for what the network needs to work.
  • EU-hosted, never sold. Data stays on servers in the European Union. No ads, no trackers.
  • Institutions keep control. A university keeps control of the data it brings and can export it at any time.
07 · For universities

Own your alumni relationship. Don't rent it.

UniCircle gives alumni offices, careers services and faculties a European, university-owned network — where alumni keep their own details current, mentor the students behind them and actually turn up when you organise something.

Talk to us about a pilot
The problem with rented networks

Your alumni are online. Your relationship with them isn't yours.

Most institutions reach their graduates through a third-party feed: a group page here, a mailing list there, a spreadsheet that was accurate the year someone graduated. The platform decides who sees your posts, and the data about your own community sits with someone else.

  • Records go stale. Graduates move cities and change jobs; the alumni database rarely hears about it.
  • Reach is rented. An algorithm you don't control decides how many alumni see your announcements.
  • Data sits elsewhere. Insight into your own community depends on another company's terms and export options.
  • Events miss their audience. Without current city information, reunions and chapters can't reliably reconnect a cohort.
  • Students and alumni stay apart. Mentoring runs on personal contacts and goodwill, so it's hard to scale beyond a few enthusiasts.

Questions

UniCircle, answered.

How much time does mentoring take?

As much as you offer. Most offers are a single call, one CV read or one coffee. You decide on each request, there's no minimum, and you can pause mentoring whenever you like.

Who can be a mentor?

Any alumnus with a UniCircle profile can switch mentoring on. You don't need a senior title — what students value most is someone who did the same programme or works in the field they're considering.

Does it cost anything?

UniCircle is free for students — including the map, your tutorial groups and reaching alumni for advice. Students never pay to reach a mentor, and alumni are never paid to mentor — it's people giving back to their own community.

What counts as a tutorial group?

The small group you meet with each week for a course — some programmes call it a seminar, workgroup or section. On UniCircle it is the course code plus the group, so classmates find each other without sharing anything else.

My city isn't on the map yet. What now?

We open city by city. Join the waitlist with the city you're in or heading to — that tells us where to open next, and you'll be on the map as soon as it does.

Does UniCircle integrate with our existing CRM?

UniCircle is designed to sit alongside your existing alumni database, not replace it. For a pilot we start with an import of the records you choose, and we'll discuss how updates should flow back to your CRM as part of scoping.

Can anyone see where I live?

No. We never ask for or store a street address. Where your dot sits, who can see it and what happens to your data are all in one place: Private by design.
